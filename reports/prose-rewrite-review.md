- Severity key: **Major** = a reader can take away the wrong claim, the wrong order, or no claim at all. **Moderate** = the logic is recoverable but only after re-reading or reconciling two passages. **Minor** = a local stumble that slows reading without changing the claim.

## 0. Overall verdicts the rewrite must preserve

1. Abstract order: **fails** problem-gap-approach-results-conclusion order. It runs problem, gap, approach fragment, then a long results list, and ends on a results clause with no separate conclusion sentence.
2. Section openings: **mixed**. Results subsections mostly open with a claim, which is good. Methods subsections mostly open with a definition or a procedure, not with the question the subsection answers. Discussion and Conclusions do not open with what they answer or establish.
3. Section closings: **mostly absent**. Only a few Results subsections close with a verdict sentence. Introduction, Methods, Discussion, and the transport, narrowing, disorder, exploratory, and follow-up subsections end without stating what they establish.
4. Transitions between sections: **mostly missing**. Abstract to Introduction, Introduction to Methods, Methods to Results, Results to Discussion, Discussion to Predictions to Limitations to Conclusions all lack a bridging sentence. Inside Results, the order of subsections also breaks the spine in two places (see C-29 and C-34).
5. The dominant failure mode is not bad sentences. It is **restatement without progression**: the same disclosure or result is stated in Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusions with no new work done at each restatement, so the reader cannot tell which occurrence is the claim and which is the reminder.

## 1. Abstract audit

The Abstract is line 33, a single paragraph. Problem is present in sentence 1. Gap is present in sentence 2 ("No strict mature-to-old test existed"). Approach is not present as a sentence: there is no sentence saying what was done (cohorts, signature, tests, window) before results begin. Results then run for the rest of the paragraph as a comma- and semicolon-joined list of numbers. There is no conclusion sentence. The spine conclusion ("aging is local and plural") never appears as a sentence in the Abstract. A reader who reads only the Abstract gets numbers without a design and without a takeaway.
