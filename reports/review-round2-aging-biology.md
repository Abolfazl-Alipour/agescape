# Hostile aging-biology review of Agescape

## Verdict

**MAJOR REVISION.** The literal title is defensible because it says that no uniform response was detected, not that shared aging mechanisms do not exist, and the manuscript repeatedly disclaims equivalence and causal inference (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:54`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:359`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:419`). I found no fatal numerical contradiction that warrants rejection. The biological framing nevertheless needs substantial correction. The paper defines uniformity so restrictively that failure is close to guaranteed, does not engage the actual modular claim made by the cited universal-hallmarks literature, treats an unstable mouse signature as evidence about cross-species biology, and makes theory distinctions that the observational data cannot discriminate. The localized heart and blood transport, replicated interneuron associations, Yazar donor-cloud spreading, and stress-score dependence support structured and cell-type-specific aging biology. They do not undermine the narrow headline, but they make the current theoretical interpretation too expansive.

## FATAL issues

None identified. The central title is an explicitly scoped detection claim, and the zebrafish and naked mole rat limitations are disclosed rather than hidden. The problems below are serious but correctable without assuming that the underlying results are invalid.

## MAJOR issues

### 1. The uniformity test attacks a stronger claim than the cited literature makes

The operational definition requires the same direction in every cell type, overlapping gene sets, comparable lifespan-scaled magnitude, and presence in every species (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:54`). That is not a necessary implication of shared hallmarks, shared upstream mechanisms, or a transportable aging clock. Tyshkovskiy and colleagues explicitly report a modular architecture and conserved signatures, not identical gene-level responses of equal size in every cell. In the same TMS resource used here, they report positive chronological-clock associations in 48 of 49 cell types and mortality-clock associations in 46 of 49 cell types (Tyshkovskiy et al., 2026, *Nature*, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10542-3). Agescape neither benchmarks those clocks nor reconciles its P1 result with that directly relevant result. Failure of one 139-gene set in the core human cohorts (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:235`) is not a general answer to that paper.

The follow-up exceptions also matter biologically. Endocardial transport passes both tests at q = 0.0150 and q = 0.0093, five Yazar blood cell types pass after adjustment, and the manuscript itself concedes that transport is therefore local rather than generally absent (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:240`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:242`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:246`). There are also 381 replicated interneuron associations (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:280`) and two Yazar cell types with donor-cloud spreading above the technical floor (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:347`). These findings do not violate an every-cell-type criterion. They show why that criterion is biologically uninformative unless contrasted with realistic alternatives such as shared modules, tissue-matched programs, and variable effect sizes.

**Fix:** Reframe the paper as evidence for heterogeneous, age-window-dependent, and cohort-dependent transcriptomic associations. If the title is retained, state immediately that it rejects only the paper's stringent operational construct. Add a direct comparison with the Tyshkovskiy chronological and mortality clocks in the same eligible TMS units, or remove any implication that P1 answers the universal-signature claim. Present heart, blood, interneuron, and cloud-spreading results as positive biology, not as residual exceptions to be rhetorically managed.

### 2. The Discussion does not discriminate among aging theories, and one species assignment is wrong

The statement that antagonistic pleiotropy and disposable soma predict "exactly this pattern" is unsupported (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:387`). Williams supplies an evolutionary account of early benefit and late harm, and Kirkwood supplies an allocation and maintenance account, but the manuscript does not derive its four transcriptomic criteria from either theory (Williams, 1957, *Evolution*, DOI: 10.2307/2406060; Kirkwood, 1977, *Nature*, DOI: 10.1038/270301a0). Heterogeneous expression is compatible with them. It is not a unique or quantitative prediction that discriminates them from hallmarks, hyperfunction, immune remodeling, or technical and cohort structure.

The hyperfunction argument is equally weak. Persistent mTOR-related activity and geroconversion do not logically require one up-minus-down mRNA signature to transport across species or equal whole-profile drift in every cell. Hyperfunction can even denote an unchanged function that is inappropriate for the post-developmental state, rather than a monotonically increasing transcriptomic readout (Blagosklonny, 2021, *Oncoscience*, DOI: 10.18632/oncoscience.545; original attribution: Blagosklonny, 2006, *Cell Cycle*, DOI: 10.4161/cc.5.18.3288). Moreover, the mature mouse result is not a decisive negative: the 18-month-reference effect is 0.0072 with p = 0.67, and the mature Bayesian 94 percent interval runs from -0.0998 to +0.1773 (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:257`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:264`). That is unresolved, not a clean refutation of continued post-maturational change.

Finally, the sentence claiming that weak continuation fits human "where drift is young-to-mature" misassigns the central young-to-mature drift finding (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:387`). That finding is mouse; human displacement is small and imprecise (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:262`). Concordance in fly and worm measures shared gene-effect directions within a cohort, not continuation of a developmental program. It cannot identify a controller or distinguish continuation from erosion, as the manuscript itself eventually admits.

**Fix:** Replace "predict exactly" with "are compatible with" throughout. Remove the claim that these data directly challenge hyperfunction unless a prespecified hyperfunction-associated developmental or mTOR program is tested after maturity. Correct the human-versus-mouse error. Do not assign species to strong or weak developmental continuation from concordance alone. For fair attribution and a real empirical benchmark, discuss the developmental-continuation literature directly, including de Magalhaes and Church, 2005, *Physiology*, DOI: 10.1152/physiol.00010.2005, and Lui et al., 2010, *Mechanisms of Ageing and Development*, DOI: 10.1016/j.mad.2010.08.010.

### 3. The Gao and Yazar power explanation is not isolated from tissue origin or signature content

The manuscript says that positive transport in the two largest single-cell follow-up cohorts is "consistent with a power explanation" (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:246`). That phrase is not false, but it is biologically under-analyzed and currently misleading. In the same Yazar cohort, under the same broad donor-level and batch-adjustment framework, P1 passes both tests in 5 of 16 cell types, whereas the blood-derived Peters signature passes in 16 of 17 benchmarked units (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:242`; `reports/followup-11pt-block10-benchmark.md:124`, `reports/followup-11pt-block10-benchmark.md:125`). Harries, another blood-derived signature, passes in 4 of 17 adjusted Yazar units (`reports/followup-11pt-block10-benchmark.md:112`, `reports/followup-11pt-block10-benchmark.md:113`). Perez blood controls are null, but that cohort has only 99 donors and is 97 percent female, so it does not cleanly separate power, transport, sex composition, and processing differences (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:87`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:242`).

The strongest biological alternative is therefore not an abstract appeal to lineage-specific aging. It is tissue-matched immunosenescence captured by blood-derived gene sets. Peters and colleagues derived their signature in whole blood, reported immune-related age pathways, and explicitly evaluated transfer across blood subsets and other tissues (Peters et al., 2015, *Nature Communications*, DOI: 10.1038/ncomms9570). The benchmark is also confounded by gene-set size, so it does not prove tissue origin. It does prove that cohort size alone is an inadequate explanation.

**Fix:** Replace the power sentence with a joint statement that donor power, tissue of derivation, gene-set size, signature stability, and cell-type composition remain competing explanations. Add donor-downsampling curves for P1 and the published signatures in the same units, plus a tissue-matched positive control in Gao, Yazar, Perez, and the core cohorts. Until then, do not privilege power over blood-specific immune aging.

### 4. P1 instability prevents the claimed clean inference about cross-species biology

P1 is method-sensitive: moderated weighted least squares calls 151 genes, DESeq2 calls 312, and unmoderated weighted least squares calls one. In leave-one-donor-out refits, 310 of 765 P1 gene-by-unit instances change sign (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:233`). The Discussion nevertheless says that transport failure has a simpler explanation "than any flaw in the signature" and is informative about cross-species biology "rather than about the quality of the original derivation" (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:389`). Those statements are incompatible with the reported derivation diagnostics.

This is not a cosmetic caveat. A gene set whose membership and direction depend strongly on estimator and donor inclusion cannot serve as a clean probe of conserved biology without a within-species positive control. The P0 comparison does not solve this problem: P0 changes unit-level outcomes but the chosen strict verdict only asks whether P0 passes where P1 fails both tests (`reports/followup-11pt-block5-p0-transport.md:116`, `reports/followup-11pt-block5-p0-transport.md:139`). That rule does not establish that either signature robustly encodes normal mouse aging.

**Fix:** Delete the claim that derivation quality is excluded. Validate P1 in held-out mouse donors and across the three derivation methods before interpreting transport biologically. Report sign stability per gene, not only gene-by-unit counts. Benchmark the Tyshkovskiy or Buckley clocks in parallel as positive controls; Buckley and colleagues demonstrated cross-species generalization for cell-type-specific clocks, a different but directly relevant design (Buckley et al., 2023, *Nature Aging*, DOI: 10.1038/s43587-022-00335-4). If P1 is unstable, transport failure remains a property of an unstable probe as well as possible species divergence.

### 5. Block 8 mislabels adult midlife transitions as maturation and treats a non-significant second step as concentration

The categorical analysis defines YOUNG as 20 to 39 years, MID as 40 to 59 years, and OLD as at least 60 years (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:179`). Calling a YOUNG-to-MID change in these adults "maturation-concentrated" is biologically wrong. It is an early-adulthood-to-midlife transition, not developmental maturation. This terminology then leaks into the developmental-continuation discussion.

The numerical pattern also does not establish that later change stops. In Yazar central memory CD4 cells, MID minus YOUNG is 1.249e-05, while OLD minus MID remains positive at 5.786e-06 with nominal p = 0.018995 and q = 0.12347 (`results/followup-11pt-block8-categorical-signature.csv:154`, `results/followup-11pt-block8-categorical-signature.csv:155`). In naive CD4 cells, the corresponding later step is 5.625e-06 with nominal p = 0.010605 and q = 0.12347 (`results/followup-11pt-block8-categorical-signature.csv:266`, `results/followup-11pt-block8-categorical-signature.csv:267`). These later estimates are approximately 46 percent and 63 percent of the earlier steps. Failure to clear BH is not evidence that the later effect is zero. The manuscript's statement that no cell type was aging-concentrated is a classification result, not proof that late-life change is absent (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:355`).

**Fix:** Rename these results "early-transition pattern under the categorical BH rule" or "young-to-midlife concentrated." Report both step sizes and intervals without implying late-step equivalence. Do not use these adult patterns as evidence for developmental continuation. If developmental maturation is the intended mechanism, use genuinely developmental donors or a validated developmental gene program.

### 6. The MSSM neuronal depth result is an age association of capture depth, not resolved neuronal biology

All four MSSM neuronal units have negative depth slopes, from -0.0560 to -0.0985 log UMI per decade, with primary q values from 0.000026 to 0.0050 (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:335`; `reports/followup-11pt-block6-depth-extension.md:100` to `reports/followup-11pt-block6-depth-extension.md:113`). The direct association is real within the fitted model. Its biological cause is not resolved. Block 6 adjusts only for age and sex, as the CSV design field also confirms (`reports/followup-11pt-block6-depth-extension.md:31`; `results/followup-11pt-block6-mssm-depth.csv:3`). It does not adjust for postmortem interval, RNA integrity, agonal state, brain bank, isolation batch, or technical principal components. The broad GABAergic unit also contains the three interneuron subtypes, so these are not four independent biological confirmations.

Postmortem effects are a credible alternative, not a demonstrated explanation. Olney and colleagues found that a 3-hour postmortem interval altered neuronal and interneuron single-nucleus expression signatures, although basic read and gene quality metrics remained stable (Olney et al., 2025, *eNeuro*, DOI: 10.1523/ENEURO.0505-24.2025). That paper does not prove that PMI causes the MSSM UMI slopes. A specific PMI explanation for these slopes is therefore **unverified**. The problem is that the manuscript likewise cannot assign them to neuronal aging. Its generic postmortem limitation (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:419`) does not specifically tell the reader that this prominent neuronal result is technically unresolved.

**Fix:** Refit with PMI, brain bank, RNA quality, processing batch, and available technical covariates if the metadata exist. Test whether age is associated with those covariates and whether the depth slopes survive. If they are unavailable, label the result explicitly as an unresolved age-associated library-depth decline and do not give it a biological interpretation. Clarify that the four units are nested.

### 7. The randomness Discussion omits the closest supportive and contradictory literature

The manuscript concludes that direct tests do not support a general rise in per-cell randomness (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:399`), but its bibliography does not engage three directly relevant papers. Levy and colleagues reported age-related loss of gene-to-gene coordination across organisms and cell types, exactly the endpoint addressed by Block 2 (Levy et al., 2020, *Nature Metabolism*, DOI: 10.1038/s42255-020-00304-4; `reports/followup-11pt-block2-gcl.md:34`). Long and colleagues reported elevated individual-level noise with age in six immune cell types using the same 981-donor OneK1K resource, exactly the dataset reanalyzed here (Long et al., 2025, *Cell Reports*, DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116612; `reports/followup-11pt-block1-residual-dispersion.md:121`). Conversely, Ibanez-Sole and colleagues found no consistent increase across seven aging datasets and emphasized method and composition dependence, directly supporting the present cautious interpretation (Ibanez-Sole et al., 2022, *eLife*, DOI: 10.7554/eLife.80380).

The Block 1 report contains a substantive reconciliation with Long through coarse-lineage pooling, finite-cell effects, and depth adjustment (`reports/followup-11pt-block1-residual-dispersion.md:304`). The manuscript omits that scientific disagreement and instead presents its own result as if it existed in a vacuum. This is not acceptable literature practice for a paper about whether transcriptional disorder is general.

**Fix:** Cite and discuss all three papers. Explain the different estimands, normalization, lineage resolution, and technical floors. State plainly that Agescape's OneK1K analysis does not reproduce Long's published age pattern after its alternative corrections, without implying that either analysis is automatically definitive. Keep donor-cloud spreading, within-cell scatter, residual dispersion, and gene coordination as separate biological endpoints.

## MINOR issues

### 1. Block 7 proves stress-score dependence, not a dying-cell or survivor-bias mechanism

The intervention removes cells above the 90th percentile of a combined apoptosis and unfolded-protein-response score (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:181`). Those Hallmark sets are not a specific assay for imminent death, and selecting high scorers from the same expression matrix does not make removal an independent mechanistic perturbation. The reported pass-to-fail changes, 77, 83, and 8 in the three Yazar types, establish sensitivity of gene-level age calls to this cell subset (`reports/followup-11pt-block7-survivor-within-type.md:134` to `reports/followup-11pt-block7-survivor-within-type.md:138`). They do not show that the removed cells were dying, that stress causes the age association, or that selective survival explains it.

The manuscript does correctly distinguish the ecological survivor-bias test and the within-type stress analysis (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:191`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:353`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:399`). That distinction should be preserved. The weaker point is the implicit mechanistic language around captured dying cells and a "lower-bound intervention."

**Fix:** Use "Hallmark-score-defined high-stress cells" consistently. Describe removal as a sensitivity analysis, not a causal intervention. Add matched-score or random-cell removal controls and independent apoptotic markers before claiming a dying-cell mechanism. Continue to state that neither Part A nor Part B proves survivor bias.

### 2. Yazar cloud spreading is not independent replication or proof of intrinsic stochasticity

Memory B and naive CD8 donor clouds spread by 0.0089008 and 0.021979 Hellinger units, respectively, above their split-half floors (`reports/followup-11pt-block3-profile-cloud.md:75`, `reports/followup-11pt-block3-profile-cloud.md:79`). That is a valid localized donor-level result. It can nevertheless reflect changing mixtures of cell states, life history, infection exposure, or other between-donor structure rather than intrinsic transcriptional randomness. The Yazar annotations lack usable internal substate resolution for these checks (`reports/followup-11pt-block1-residual-dispersion.md:267`).

The prediction section calls naive CD8 scatter and variance widening evidence "across independent tests" (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:407`). These are distinct estimands applied to the same cohort, not independent biological replication.

**Fix:** Say "distinct analyses of the same Yazar cohort." Do not equate donor-cloud spreading with per-cell stochasticity. Replicate in an independent blood cohort and test finer cell-state composition before assigning a biological noise mechanism.

### 3. The ordered-vulnerability interpretation of interneurons exceeds the temporal evidence

The manuscript calls the three interneuron subtypes those that "change most and earliest" and proposes a preclinical footprint of an ordered vulnerability sequence (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:391`). The analysis reports cross-sectional effect correlations and replicated gene counts, not onset ages or a formal ordering test (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:280`). Gabitto and colleagues support early SST vulnerability in Alzheimer's disease, but that disease sequence cannot by itself establish that normal age-associated expression in MSSM and HBCC is preclinical Alzheimer's disease (Gabitto et al., 2024, *Nature Neuroscience*, DOI: 10.1038/s41593-024-01774-5). SEA-AD did not replicate the program, and mature cross-cohort replication is unavailable (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:282`; `reports/followup-11pt-block9-mature-interneuron.md:5`).

**Fix:** Remove "earliest" and "same order as our finding" unless onset is modeled explicitly. Present the disease connection as a hypothesis motivated by shared subtypes, not as a demonstrated trajectory from normal aging to Alzheimer's disease.

### 4. Shared cell-type names are caveated correctly, but lifespan scaling still implies an unjustified biological comparison

The manuscript appropriately states that shared annotation names do not prove evolutionary matches (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:383`). That is consistent with cell-type evolutionary frameworks that require more than nomenclature to establish homology (Arendt et al., 2016, *Nature Reviews Genetics*, DOI: 10.1038/nrg.2016.127). The main concordance analysis is within species, so it does not itself establish cross-species conservation or mismatch.

The remaining problem is the use of 10 percent of maximum lifespan as a component of comparability (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:54`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:138`). Maximum-lifespan scaling does not make mouse, human, fly, worm, zebrafish, and naked mole rat trajectories developmentally or physiologically equivalent. The manuscript later concedes that standardized effects are not directly comparable across species (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:442`).

**Fix:** Keep the explicit naming caveat and do not describe within-species concordance as evolutionary conservation. Treat lifespan scaling as a descriptive normalization, not a biological equivalence criterion for uniformity. Report age windows and covered life-history stages alongside every cross-species comparison.

### 5. Zebrafish and naked mole rat caveats are honest, but their results remain too prominent in the headline accounting

The zebrafish caveat is unusually clear: testis only, six fish in the fine analysis, and 97.23 percent germline cells (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:134`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:427`). The naked mole rat arms are also handled honestly: five skin animals, exact p = 0.2, and seven spleen animals fully confounded by colony (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:373`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:425`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:451`). I find no concealed overclaim of age resistance or transcriptomic stability.

However, the abstract and primary-family narrative lead with 45 composition passes without immediately separating the 17 core passes from 28 added fly, worm, and zebrafish passes (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:37`, `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:218`). The same abstract lists zebrafish and naked mole rat non-detection beside better-powered mouse and human tests (`report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex:37`). That presentation gives exploratory and sometimes confounded arms more evidential visual weight than they deserve.

**Fix:** Report core and exploratory composition counts separately in the abstract. Keep zebrafish germline and naked mole rat colony limitations adjacent to any cross-species result, not only in Limitations. Do not use these nulls as evidence for species differences or absence of aging effects.
