# Hostile review, round 2: aging biology, theory, and literature

Manuscript: `report/plos-one/final-plos-one.tex`, 721 lines, at commit `683a74d` (tag `mature-to-old-rewrite-2026-11-21`), repo `agescape-revision`.
Reviewer stance: hostile aging-biology reviewer at a top-tier bar (Nature Aging / Nature / Cell standard), benchmarked against 2024 to 2026 literature.
Nothing is submitted anywhere. The tex was not edited.

## Verdict: MAJOR REVISION

The descriptive core of this paper is real and valuable: a well powered, donor-level, mature-to-old test that finds no uniform transport of a mouse aging signature, an unstable signature that barely overlaps its young-to-old predecessor, and pervasive heterogeneity. The Discussion is unusually honest about most of this. But the paper as written cannot go out at a top-tier bar, because two of its three headline narrow signals fail the repo's own robustness checks and are overstated in the abstract, and because the title and abstract claim more than the paper's own operational construct and probe can support.

At a Nature Aging bar, without the fixes below, this is a reject on framing and on the two T cell signals. With the fixes, it is a strong major revision. It is not a reject on the underlying work.

## The three issues that matter most

1. The naive CD8 scatter signal collapses when log captured cell count is added as a covariate in the repo's own CSV (slope 0.0080, p 0.526, q 0.727, against a primary slope of 0.0498, p 5.92e-05, q 4.74e-04), flips sign without pool fixed effects, and dies at 20-cell subsampling. The tex reports it as a survived signal and never mentions these arms. See BLOCKER-1.
2. The naive CD4 dispersion signal survives the frozen rule but not a mechanistic reading: the repo's own Block 1 report concludes it is "not proof of a biological dispersion rise", with null unadjusted (p 0.814) and rarefied (p 0.797) arms, 56.4 percent of the slope explained by finite-cell calibration, and log cell count falling steeply with age. The abstract presents it as a survived signal. See BLOCKER-2.
3. The title and abstract test a straw version of "uniform" that no current theory requires, on a probe the Discussion itself calls unstable and sex-confounded, while the two genes the field's strongest 2026 universality paper names (CDKN1A, LGALS3) were never directly tested: CDKN1A is absent from the mature signature and LGALS3 reverses direction in it. See MAJOR-1, MAJOR-2, MAJOR-6.

## A. Theory: the construct tests a straw version of "uniform"

### MAJOR-1. The opening sentence and the title state a claim no current theory makes

Title, line 21: "No uniform transcriptional response detected in mouse and human aging". Abstract, line 37: "Aging is described as one process with one cause, yet organs and cell types age differently."

No current theory describes aging as one process with one cause. The hallmarks framework, which the paper itself cites (line 44, ref 30), lists twelve shared mechanisms, from genomic instability to chronic inflammation, and requires each hallmark to appear with age, to accelerate aging when accentuated, and to slow aging when targeted. Antagonistic pleiotropy, disposable soma, and hyperfunction (lines 44, 337) are all multi-cause frameworks. The paper's own Discussion concedes the point twice: line 337 states the four criteria "are not unique quantitative predictions" of any theory named, and "the present observational data cannot discriminate these theoretical accounts or identify a controller"; line 54 states the construct is "deliberately more stringent than shared hallmarks, modular conserved signatures, or transportable clocks" and that "a shared upstream process whose consequences differ by cell type would fail these criteria by design, and it is not excluded by this paper."

So the paper is rigorous in the Discussion and overreaches in the title and abstract. A reader who reads only the title, which is most readers, will take this as a verdict on the hallmarks and on universal aging mechanisms. The paper's own text says it is not that. Fix: qualify the title to the construct actually tested, for example "No uniform mature-to-old transcriptional response detected under a stringent four-criterion construct", and replace the abstract's opening sentence with an accurate statement of what the theories actually claim. The current opening is a strawman and a top-tier reviewer will call it one in the first paragraph.

### MAJOR-2. Criterion 1 (same direction in EVERY cell type) makes the negative conclusion near-tautological

Line 54 defines the construct: same direction in every cell type tested, overlapping gene sets, comparable magnitude after lifespan scaling, presence in each species. Criterion 1 alone guarantees failure in any dataset with more than a handful of cell types, because any shared program will have at least one cell type where it is absent, weak, or reversed for local reasons. The paper's own literature review contains the counterexamples. Line 48 cites a re-analysis of the mouse atlas finding "330 genes changing with age in over half of all tissue-cell types, a shared core that changes congruently" (ref 39), and Jeffries and colleagues finding 124 genes commonly downregulated across multiple brain cell types (ref 40, see MAJOR-5 for the count error). Both are shared cores that fail criterion 1 only because they are not present in literally every type. A construct that classifies a 330-gene shared core and a 124-gene shared core as "no uniform response" is testing the word "every", not the biology. The paper concedes this in line 54 and line 337, but the title, abstract (line 37), Results heading (line 253), and Conclusions (line 411) all state the negative result without the qualifier. Fix: either soften criterion 1 to a defined majority or prevalence threshold with a stated rationale, or keep the strict criterion and state in the abstract, not only the Discussion, that the negative result is a statement about the strict construct and is compatible with shared cores of the size the literature already reports.

### MAJOR-3. No pathway-level uniformity test is reported, although the field's shared signals are pathway-level

Criterion 2 requires "overlapping gene sets driving the change, not only correlated directions" (line 54). Every test in the paper operationalizes this at gene identity level: slope concordance, shared false-discovery genes, signature transport of a fixed gene list. But the shared signals the recent literature reports are pathway-level, not gene-identity-level. Tyshkovskiy and colleagues report conserved modules (inflammation, interferon signalling, mitochondrial function, chromatin modification, extracellular matrix), not identical gene lists in every cell (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10542-3). Dobner and colleagues report a pan-endothelial program of rising inflammation, declining angiogenesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, and a p21-linked senescence program (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919924/). Sloan and colleagues report a shared decline in oxidative phosphorylation and cytoplasmic translation genes across dorsolateral prefrontal cortex cell types (https://www.cell.com/cell-genomics/fulltext/S2666-979X(25)00383-0). Jeffries and colleagues report shared downregulation of short housekeeping genes for ribosomes, transport, and metabolism (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09435-8). A gene-level null does not rule out pathway-level uniformity, and the paper never runs the test that would: per-cell-type age gene lists, Gene Ontology or pathway enrichment per type, then an overlap test of enriched pathways across types. This is the single most important missing analysis. It is also cheap: the per-type differential expression results already exist. Fix: run a pathway-level concordance test across cell types within each cohort, report it with the same rigor as the gene-level concordance, and scope the headline claim to the level actually tested if the pathway test is not run.

## B. Literature: omissions and selective citation against 2024 to 2026 work

### MAJOR-4. Dobner 2026 is cited only for the half that supports the paper

Line 48: "And within a single human cell lineage, endothelial cells age differently by tissue and vascular bed [42]." Dobner and colleagues, Nature Aging 2026, built the Human Aging Endothelial Cell Atlas from over 375,000 endothelial cells from 290 donors across 12 human tissues (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919924/). Their headline finding is a conserved pan-endothelial aging program: rising inflammation and senescence-associated secretory phenotype factors, declining angiogenic capacity (notably in venous endothelial cells), widespread extracellular matrix and mechanotransduction remodeling, and a p21-linked senescence program validated in vitro and in vivo. Tissue specificity is the second half of their result, not the first. The manuscript cites Dobner only for specificity and never mentions the conserved program, even though Dobner is the strongest 2026 counterexample to the paper's framing inside a single lineage, and even though the paper's own composition analysis promotes mouse lung vein endothelial cells as one of its two headline fraction tests (line 245). Worse, Dobner's central gene is CDKN1A (p21), which is absent from P1m (see MAJOR-6). Fix: engage Dobner's conserved program directly, test the Dobner pan-endothelial gene set for transport in the paper's endothelial units, and report the result either way.

### MAJOR-5. The Jeffries "124 genes" count is wrong on "all", and the scope limit is buried

Lines 48 and 341: "124 genes fall across all cell types with age, mostly housekeeping genes for translation and metabolism, while neuron-specific genes stay stable [40]." Jeffries and colleagues, Nature 2025, report 124 genes commonly downregulated across multiple cell types, but only 10 genes significantly downregulated in all 13 brain cell types (HSPA8, TUBA1A, VAMP2, and seven others) (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09435-8). The manuscript's "124 genes fall across all cell types" conflates the multi-type set with the all-13 set. That is a citation accuracy error, and it matters because the paper's criterion 1 turns on the word "all". Second, Jeffries' shared signal compares 7 elderly donors with 10 adult donors across infancy to centenarian lifespan, and the authors link the housekeeping downregulation to somatic mutation accumulation that is gene-length and expression dependent, that is, to DNA damage as a shared upstream mechanism producing a shared transcriptional direction. That cuts directly against the paper's framing: a shared upstream process (DNA damage) producing a shared direction (housekeeping downregulation) in the same tissue (human prefrontal cortex) the paper studies. The paper's mature-to-old window cannot see this signal, because Jeffries' contrast includes young-to-mature change. The manuscript never states this scope limit in the abstract. Fix: correct the count to 124 multi-type and 10 all-13, and state in the abstract that the negative result is conditional on the mature-to-old window and does not address full-lifespan shared signals of the Jeffries type.

### MAJOR-6. Tyshkovskiy 2026 is never actually tested, and its two named genes fail in P1m

Line 54 states: "We did not benchmark their chronological or mortality clocks, so the P1 analysis neither confirms nor refutes that distinct claim." That sentence is honest. The problem is that the title will be read as a rebuttal of Tyshkovskiy and colleagues, Nature 2026 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10542-3), which reports universal transcriptomic hallmarks of mammalian aging and mortality from over 11,000 transcriptomes across 25 tissues and 4 mammals, with modular architecture and named universal features including CDKN1A and LGALS3, whose protein levels tracked mortality and multimorbidity in UK Biobank. I checked the mature signature gene list (`results/followup-mature-p1m-genes.csv`) and the P1 to P1m overlap file (`results/followup-mature-p1m-p1-overlap.csv`) directly. CDKN1A (mouse Cdkn1a) was UP in the young-to-old signature P1 and is lost from P1m entirely. LGALS3 (mouse Lgals3) was UP in P1 and is present in P1m only as DOWN (mean log2 fold change minus 2.71, 1 unit), recorded in the overlap file as a common sign flip. So of the two genes Tyshkovskiy names as universal features, one is absent from the probe and the other reverses direction in it. The paper never tests the Tyshkovskiy clock genes or modules as a set, never tests CDKN1A or LGALS3 directly, and never asks whether the inflammation and interferon modules that Tyshkovskiy reports as conserved are present in its own data. Some module genes are in P1m (IRF7, ISG15, APOE, C1QA are all UP), which makes the omission worse: the data to run a module-level test exist and were not used. Fix: run a direct test of the Tyshkovskiy universal gene set and modules for transport and for direction consistency, report it, and qualify the title so it cannot be read as a rebuttal of a paper the authors state they did not test.

### MAJOR-7. Sloan 2026, same tissue as the paper's brain cohorts, is used only as a motivation citation

Line 341: "Disease studies motivate attention to these subtypes [46, 47], but cross-sectional age slopes in two brain banks cannot establish a preclinical Alzheimer's disease sequence." Reference 46 is Sloan and colleagues, Cell Genomics 2026, "Uncovering the signatures of aging and senescence in the human dorsolateral prefrontal cortex" (https://www.cell.com/cell-genomics/fulltext/S2666-979X(25)00383-0). Sloan is not a disease-motivation citation. It is a direct aging study in the exact tissue (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex) of the paper's PsychAD and MSSM cohorts, and its three headline findings all bear on the paper's claims. First, SST transcript falls sharply and SST-positive neuron density falls with age, which is a composition change that interacts directly with the paper's interneuron replication story (see MAJOR-13) and with its MSSM SST depth result (see MAJOR-10). Second, nuclei from dorsolateral prefrontal cortex cell types show a strong shared decline in oxidative phosphorylation and cytoplasmic translation genes with age, which is a pathway-level shared direction of exactly the kind MAJOR-3 says was never tested. Third, oligodendrocytes show senescence hallmarks and a linear CDKN2A increase with age, while CDKN2A is absent from P1m. None of these three findings is discussed anywhere in the manuscript. Fix: engage Sloan's findings directly, test whether the SST density loss explains any part of the interneuron or depth results, and report the shared oxidative phosphorylation and translation decline against the paper's uniformity criteria.

### MAJOR-8. Yang 2026, the source paper for the paper's own brain atlas, is not reconciled with Block 8

The PsychAD lifespan cortex arm is the paper's primary human brain dataset (line 72, ref 9). Its source paper, Yang and colleagues, Nature 2026, reports a single-nucleus transcriptomic atlas of 284 neurotypical donors and 1,307,674 nuclei, identifies three major phases of molecular change across the lifespan, and finds late-life glial programs associated with immune activation, stress responses, and circadian reorganization (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10271-7). Yang also reports that age explains the most transcriptional variance during development (mean 4 percent) and the second largest effect in late adulthood (mean 2 percent). The manuscript's Block 8 categorical test finds no early-transition labels and no regime contrast (lines 405, 337). Those two results sit in the same manuscript without any reconciliation: Yang finds phased molecular change in the same atlas; Agescape finds no categorical transition. They may be compatible (phases are not step changes), but a reviewer will demand the explicit comparison, including whether Yang's late-life glial programs appear in the paper's own PsychAD units. Fix: add a short subsection reconciling Block 8 with Yang's three-phase structure in the same data, and state explicitly that the mature-to-old window discards the developmental variance component where Yang finds the largest age effect.

### MAJOR-9. Tabula Sapiens 2.0 is cited as a preprint although it is now published, and its own senescence analysis is ignored

Line 72 cites Tabula Sapiens 2.0 with "preprint version 2024.12.03.626516v1". Tabula Sapiens 2.0 is now published in Cell, 2026, 189:7212-7227 (https://www.cell.com/cell/pdf/S0092-8674(26)00937-2.pdf), 1.1 million cells from 24 donors across 28 tissues. Update the citation. More substantively, the published Tabula Sapiens 2.0 paper's aging analysis finds that CDKN2A-positive senescence phenotypes differ markedly by cell type and tissue, and that senescent cells share common gene expression modules alongside organ- and cell-specific patterns. That is a shared program plus specificity, which is the same duality the paper reports, and it comes from the same resource the paper analyzes. The manuscript's Tabula Sapiens 2.0 result, line 325, is "no gene passing correction in 688,294 tests" on nine donors. Nine donors cannot test a shared senescence program; that is a power statement, not a substantive null, and the manuscript should say so rather than letting the zero stand as a finding. Fix: update the citation, cite the published senescence analysis, and label the nine-donor Tabula Sapiens 2.0 null as underpowered in Results, not only in Limitations.

### MAJOR-10. Buckley clocks generalize across species and were never benchmarked

Line 339 concedes: "cell-type-specific clocks trained in mouse brain can generalize across species [45], but age prediction is a different task from transporting this fixed gene set. We did not perform held-out-mouse validation across derivation methods or benchmark those clocks." Buckley and colleagues, Nature Aging 2023, trained single-cell aging clocks in mouse subventricular zone and showed generalization to independent mouse cohorts, other brain regions, and other species including humans (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37118510/). That is a direct, published demonstration that a mouse-derived transcriptomic aging signal transports across species, using a per-cell-type clock trained the way the field trains clocks. The paper's transport test uses a fixed 1,914-gene set derived by a different procedure from three organs, with no held-out validation. The transport failure is therefore a property of this probe, and the paper says so in the Discussion, but the title and abstract present it as a property of mouse-to-human aging. Fix: either benchmark a Buckley-style per-cell-type clock on the same cohorts, or state in the abstract that transport failure was shown only for this fixed signature and that published cross-species clocks were not tested. At minimum the abstract must carry that qualifier.

## C. The three narrow signals

### BLOCKER-1. The naive CD8 scatter signal collapses under log cell-count adjustment, and the tex never reports that arm

Line 273 reports naive CD8 T cells in Yazar blood as a survived signal: within-cell scatter rises with age, standard deviation slope 0.0498 per decade, p 5.92e-05, q 4.74e-04, 344 donors, 17,878 cells. I verified the primary row in `results/followup-mature-fulldepth-yazar-cell-drift.csv`: slope 0.0498, p 5.92e-05, q 4.74e-04. I also verified the sensitivity arms in the same file, which the tex never mentions. Adding log total cells as a covariate (`plus_log_ncells`) kills the signal completely: slope 0.0080, p 0.526, q 0.727. Removing pool fixed effects (`no_pool`) flips it negative: slope minus 0.0312, p 0.131. Twenty-cell subsampling kills it: slope minus 0.0016, p 0.887. It survives rarefaction at 1500 (slope 0.0885, p 5.5e-06) and spline residuals (slope 0.0925, p 6.9e-05), but a signal that exists only inside one covariate specification, reverses sign without pool fixed effects, and vanishes when cell count is added is not a robust biological finding. It is a cell-count and pooling artifact until proven otherwise.

Three facts make the artifact reading the default. First, the paper's own Methods (line 184) state that cell-count-adjusted checks "are binding on robustness: a nominal positive rule pass without their support is inconclusive", for the sibling residual-dispersion estimand. The same logic applies to scatter, the data exist, the signal fails, and the tex does not report it. Second, Yazar captured cell counts per donor fall about 2.7 percent per decade and that decline survives pool adjustment (p 6.9e-08, `results/csv-tests-2026-11-17/depth-vs-age/depth-vs-age-report.md`), so the confounder is real and age-associated in the same cohort. Third, the established immunology literature offers a ready alternative: surviving naive CD8 cells in old age preferentially acquire a memory-like phenotype and are selected for high T cell receptor avidity under homeostatic proliferation (Venturi and colleagues, PNAS 2011, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1107594108), and naive CD4 cells are maintained by peripheral division with accumulating clonal expansions (Egorov and colleagues, Frontiers in Immunology 2018). The "naive CD8" gate in old donors is therefore increasingly contaminated by memory-like, clonally expanded cells, which is a composition and identity shift, not increased per-cell transcriptional randomness. The manuscript never addresses gate contamination. Fix before submission: report the `plus_log_ncells`, `no_pool`, and subsampling arms in Results next to the primary, downgrade the naive CD8 scatter signal to inconclusive, and remove it from the abstract's list of survived signals unless a cell-count-independent replication is produced.

### BLOCKER-2. The naive CD4 dispersion signal survives the frozen rule but not its own report's mechanistic reading

Line 277 reports naive CD4 T cells in Yazar blood as the one proving type: slope plus 0.00071 residual dispersion units per decade, p 5.174e-08, q 8.279e-07, 777 donors. I verified the primary row in `results/followup-mature-block1-residual-dispersion.csv`: slope 0.0007139, p 5.174e-08, q 8.279e-07, n 777. The primary number is real. The interpretation is not. The repo's own Block 1 report (`reports/followup-mature-block1.md`, lines 228 to 232) is more skeptical than the tex: finite-N calibration accounts for 56.4 percent of the primary slope; the debiased slope that remains (0.000313, p 0.000638) sits on top of a still-present log cell-fraction coupling; and the report concludes "That is not proof of a biological dispersion rise; it is a weaker calibration story than the original had, and the verdict rests on the frozen rule, not on a resolved mechanism." The same report records that the unadjusted arm is null (slope 0.00003, p 0.8142), the rarefied-1500 arm is null (slope 0.00008, p 0.7971), the gene-fraction arm is null (p 0.1529), and the shared-trend arm is null (p 0.7370). I verified the unadjusted and rarefied rows directly in the CSV. The signal also survives adding log cell count (0.000286, p 0.00660) and cell count plus depth (0.00024, p 0.01893), which is the strongest point in its favor, but log cell count itself falls steeply with age in naive CD4 cells (minus 0.0897 per decade, p 8.40e-08) and even faster in naive CD8 cells (minus 0.2097, p 6.90e-16), and the outcome correlates strongly and negatively with log cell count (partial correlation minus 0.625). A dispersion signal that is null without batch adjustment, null under rarefaction, and half explained by finite-cell calibration, in a cell type whose captured count falls steeply with age, is not a finding a top-tier journal will accept as a headline result. Fix before submission: report the null arms and the calibration fraction in Results, downgrade the abstract's "three narrow signals survived" to name only signals that survive their own calibration, and either resolve the mechanism or present naive CD4 dispersion as an unresolved candidate, not a finding.

### MAJOR-11. MSSM depth decline is a sex-only model on four nested units, and the abstract drops the qualifier the Conclusions carry

Line 37 lists "four MSSM neuron types, whose captured library depth fell with age" as one of three survived signals. Line 411 qualifies it: "captured-library-depth decline in four nested MSSM neuron types with unresolved technical explanations." The abstract carries no such qualifier. I verified the numbers in `results/followup-mature-block6-mssm-depth.csv`: GABA minus 0.1039 per decade, q 2.76e-05, n 153; VIP minus 0.0877, q 6.74e-05, n 193; parvalbumin minus 0.0839, q 3.65e-04, n 185; somatostatin minus 0.0524, q 0.0064, n 174. The effect is real in the data. But the model covariates are intercept, age, and sex only. No postmortem interval, no brain bank, no batch, no RNA quality metric, no technical principal components. Postmortem brain cohorts are exactly where those variables correlate with donor age, and depth per nucleus is the single most technical outcome in the paper. Second, the four units are nested: GABAergic neurons contain the VIP, parvalbumin, and somatostatin subtypes, so this is effectively one finding reported four times. Third, Sloan 2026 finds SST-positive neuron density falls with age in human dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (MAJOR-7), which offers a composition and selection alternative for the SST depth result that the paper never tests. Fourth, if depth per nucleus genuinely falls with age in MSSM neurons, that technical variable touches every MSSM per-cell analysis in the paper, including the interneuron replication. Fix: add postmortem interval, bank, batch, and RNA quality covariates to the depth model and report whether the decline survives; state the nesting explicitly in Results and count it as one signal, not four; and carry the "unresolved technical explanations" qualifier into the abstract.

## D. Transport: microglia and GTEx whole blood

### MAJOR-12. Microglia-only transport is marginal, sign-flipping, and never decomposed

Line 261: only two core units pass both transport tests under P1m, PsychAD microglia (composite q 0.00457, empirical q 0.0135) and MSSM microglial cells (composite q 0.0497, empirical q 0.00262), both in the negative direction. Three problems. First, the MSSM composite q of 0.0497 is marginal, one rounding step from null. Second, the PsychAD microglia slope flips sign between windows in the same cohort: plus 0.002381 per year full-range, minus 0.001786 mature unadjusted (line 267). A signature score that reverses direction when the age window changes in the same data is not a stable biological signal; it is what an unstable probe produces. Third, the literature says microglia do have a conserved aging program, and the paper never tests it. Disease-associated and lipid-associated microglia states are conserved across mouse and human, with shared transcriptional regulators including BHLHE40 and BHLHE41 (Podlesny-Drabiniok and colleagues, Nature Communications 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46315-7), and aging-associated white-matter microglia activation is a reproducible 2025 finding (Groh and colleagues, Nature Neuroscience 2025). The paper never tests the disease-associated microglia gene set for transport. Most sharply, the paper's own marker panel (line 477) reports increased microglial C1QA in MSSM (slope plus 0.00522, q 0.000280), and C1QA is in P1m as UP, yet the P1m composite in microglia falls with age. The paper never decomposes the composite to say which genes drive the negative slope, so a reader cannot tell whether the negative transport is biology or an artifact of the 1,914-gene mixture. Fix: test the disease-associated microglia gene set directly, decompose the microglia composite by gene, and report the sign flip prominently in Results rather than only in the PsychAD paragraph. The microglia result as written cannot carry the weight of being the paper's only positive transport.

### MAJOR-13. GTEx whole blood, the one positive transport, is bulk and most parsimoniously composition

Line 265: "Track 2 whole blood is the one positive both-pass (plus 0.0119 per decade bin under P1m, q 1.29e-22, empirical q 0.0015)". I verified the numbers in `results/followup-mature-organ-transports-c-gtex.csv`: effect plus 0.01194, q 1.29e-22, empirical q 0.0015; P0m plus 0.01219, q 2.45e-20. The paper itself notes "Bulk shifts conflate within-cell aging with cell-type proportion shifts" (line 265). That note is correct and insufficient. The most parsimonious explanation is composition: naive T cells fall with age (the paper's own Thomson PBMC result, line 273: naive CD8 share centered log ratio minus 1.319, p 0.000444, q 0.00578) and myeloid cells rise, so a bulk whole-blood score moves because the mixture changes, not because any cell ages. The paper never tests this, although the test is straightforward: deconvolve the GTEx whole-blood samples with the paper's own PBMC fractions, or adjust the GTEx score for estimated naive T and monocyte proportions, and report whether the positive transport survives. Until that test is run, the paper's single positive transport is also its least interpretable result, and the abstract should not present the transport story as uniformly negative while leaving the one positive unexplained. Fix: run the composition-adjusted GTEx whole-blood test, or explicitly downgrade the whole-blood result to a bulk-composition candidate in Results and Conclusions.

## E. Interneuron replication and TRMT11

### MAJOR-14. TRMT11 is statistically real and biologically near-empty

Lines 281 and 341: interneuron expression replicates across MSSM and HBCC with TRMT11 as the only shared gene passing false discovery rate in all three subtypes (VIP, parvalbumin, somatostatin) in both cohorts on the unmoderated test. I verified the rows in `results/followup-mature-interneuron-genes.csv`: TRMT11 is both-FDR in all three subtypes in both cohorts, same direction, slopes 0.00205 to 0.00229 log counts per 10k per year in MSSM and 0.00280 to 0.00406 in HBCC. The replication is statistically real. But the effect is tiny, about 2 to 4 percent per decade in a gene expressed at about 4 counts per 10k and detected in 100 percent of cells, and TRMT11 has no established aging biology. TRMT11 is a tRNA methyltransferase (with TRMT112, it makes m2G10 in cytoplasmic and mitochondrial tRNAs, affecting decoding of mitochondrial mRNAs MT-ND3, MT-ND6, MT-CO1, MT-CO2, MT-CO3), and Trmt11 knockout mice show no defects in development, growth, heart, muscle, or spermatogenesis (https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/21/gkag1176/9089559). No aging link and no disease association were found. The paper's Prediction 1 (line 355) says to test TRMT11 first, which is honest about it being a starting point, but a single housekeeping tRNA-modification enzyme with a 2 to 4 percent per decade slope is what a 1,800-gene screen produces by chance once, and the paper gives no reason to believe it is anything else. Two further facts weaken the framing. The full-range analysis found six shared genes (FBN1, GABRB1, IGF1R, PHF3, TRMT11, ZBTB16, line 455); the mature restriction collapses six to one, which is a statement about the window, not about biology. And SEA-AD dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, 15 donors without dementia, did not replicate the program (line 281), so generalizability is limited to two cohorts. Fix: present TRMT11 as a replicated statistical association of unknown significance, not as a lead, and either give a mechanistic reason to prioritize it or drop Prediction 1 in favor of a prediction about the replicated subtype-level correlations, which are the actual finding.

## F. Title, abstract, and the 624 family

### MAJOR-15. "Locked" in the abstract contradicts the Methods on family selection timing

Line 37 (abstract): "The locked 624-test composition family yielded 45 passes". Lines 143 and 245 (Methods and Results): "Both the 431-test and 624-test outputs were produced without selecting either one, and the choice of primary family was deferred until after the mammalian p values and both correction outcomes were available. The 624-test family is therefore the declared primary policy for this report, not a preregistered or outcome-blind scope." And: "The 624-test family was selected after both correction outcomes were available, and its experiment-wide rationale does not remove that selection timing." Line 397 (Limitations) repeats the disclosure. The word "locked" in the abstract implies a fixed, outcome-blind choice. The Methods state the opposite. Both promoted tests pass only in the full family (mouse lung vein endothelial: core-only q 0.0690, 624-family q 0.0390; human kidney collecting duct: core-only q 0.0667, 624-family q 0.0377) and both fail sensitivity checks (total-cell-adjusted q 0.0627 and 0.4231; propeller p 0.0528 and 0.0588, line 245). The body discloses all of this carefully; the abstract undoes it with one word. Fix: remove "locked" from the abstract and state the selection timing there in one clause. A reviewer who finds an abstract-to-Methods contradiction on multiplicity will distrust the rest of the multiplicity handling.

## Verdict restated

MAJOR REVISION on biology and literature grounds. The paper's central descriptive contribution, a rigorous mature-to-old null for uniform transport plus a demonstrated unstable signature, is sound and worth publishing. But the abstract currently sells three narrow signals, two of which fail the repo's own robustness checks, under a title that tests a construct no theory requires on a probe the paper itself calls unstable and sex-confounded, while the strongest recent universality claims in the literature go untested and partly unmentioned. Fix the two T cell signals, qualify the title and abstract, run the three direct gene-set tests, and engage Dobner, Sloan, Yang, and Jeffries at their actual claims, and this becomes a paper a top-tier reviewer can take seriously.
