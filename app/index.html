Finding: within mouse; cross-species conservation not established

We searched every cell type in the aging mouse atlas for genes that change with age. Only 17 of 163 cell-type units met the eligibility floor; 14 show aging-associated differential expression, all in lung and kidney. The catalog contains 261 direction-specific gene entries across 257 unique genes, with limited overlap among significant lists. In human analyses of 621 public donors and a 284-donor lifespan atlas, whole-cohort summaries provide no supported evidence of a rise. This page presents the evidence, its statistical limits, and designs that could improve inference.

The story in 30 seconds

  1. 1 Where can aging even be measured? Only 17 of 163 mouse cell-type units met the eligibility floor. All 17 are in lung and kidney, but meeting the floor does not establish adequate power.
  2. 2 Is the signal real? The top unit shows 55 aging-associated genes against a mean of 4.6 in five permutations, and its effect estimates agree with the published atlas. The small permutation check is supportive, not precise calibration.
  3. 3 What is the aging signature? The catalog has 261 direction-specific entries across 257 unique genes. Significant lists overlap little, but that alone does not prove cell-type-private biology.
  4. 4 Does it hold across species? Mouse scores rise in a partly in-sample analysis. Five naked mole-rats cannot establish either change or stability. Human whole-cohort summaries show no supported rise, while cell-type estimates vary by cohort and normalization.
  5. 5 What would improve human inference? More donors with broader age coverage can reduce detectable-effect sizes, but the illustrative grid does not guarantee power or prescribe a design that works. The 284-donor lifespan atlas provides a complementary analysis.

Three checks before interpreting the numbers: compare with shuffled labels, compare with published estimates, and examine the relationship with cell maturation.

Pipeline direction matches the published atlas in 13 of 17 units (4 partial, 0 disagree)

Agreement with the TMS paper's published age coefficients across the 17 eligible units: 13 agree, 4 partial, 0 disagree. Calibration check: 55 age-differentially expressed genes observed versus a mean of 4.6 across five permutations

Observed in the top unit (lung adventitial cell), 3 month vs 18 month: 55 age-differentially expressed genes. Five label permutations give a mean null count of 4.6, a coarse comparison rather than a precise null estimate. The within-3-month sex comparison (2 female vs 5 male mice) finds 0 genes at FDR < 0.05, but this small comparison does not rule out sex effects or establish that the aging signal is independent of sex.

Housekeeping guard: Ribosomal and mitochondrial transcript inflation

Ribosomal (Rpl/Rps) and mitochondrial (mt-) genes represent a large fraction of raw single-cell sequencing reads. Across the 17 units, housekeeping genes account for 1.7% of background genes and make up as much as 10% of one unit's program (kidney fenestrated cell). These genes are monitored rather than filtered out of the reported programs; a high fraction is a composition caveat, not automatic evidence of an artifact.

Savina re-analysis: converted natural-log cutoff gives 9 nominal and 6 FDR DE genes

Savina et al. 2022 (Aging) reported zero global DE genes between young and aged naked mole-rat epidermis at their stated rule. If the stated cutoff is interpreted in natural-log units and converted to absolute log2FC 1.45, we find 9 genes at nominal p < 0.05 and 6 at FDR < 0.05. Using the literal absolute log2FC cutoff 1.0 gives 29 nominal genes. A units mismatch is one possible explanation, not an established cause. These are cell-level Wilcoxon tests on five animals, not animal-replicated DE, and they do not establish overall age-related stability. Additional caveats: Igfbp3, the paper's key gene, is absent from the GEO count file and could not be checked; our QC retained 10,251 cells vs the paper's 10,232.

How to read the cell-type evidence

Large dots and intervals use sum-count pseudobulk; small dots use counts per million (CPM). Solid means same-direction nominal p < 0.05 in both schemes, not uniform robustness. Six of seven also have BH q < 0.05 in both. Adaptive does not (CPM q = 0.092) and is not significant in adults only (p = 0.127). Hover or tap a row for both q values and the adults-only result.

Donors with zero cells of a type are excluded from that row. Missing cell types are not biological zeros. Intervals that include zero do not establish neuronal flatness or equivalence. Control means no psychiatric or neurological diagnosis; common medical comorbidities remain.

What would improve human inference?

In the illustrative G13 grid, donor-age SD 12 years and dispersion 0.1 give a minimum detectable 30-year log2FC of 0.491 at 16 donors. At dispersion 0.2, the grid gives a comparable value of 0.521 at 16 donors and SD 16 years. At dispersion 0.4, it gives 0.556 at 50 donors and SD 12 years. These are modeled detectable effects, not guaranteed designs. The DLPFC Lifespan Atlas in G16 is a complementary dataset with 284 donors and age SD 24, not validation of the grid or a universal design.

2. Human Data Coverage

The human arm is tested in 11 public CELLxGENE cohorts (621 control donors) and the 284-donor DLPFC Lifespan Atlas under a data use agreement. A query of the SenNet public index did not identify unrestricted datasets with donor ages in that search. The Tabula Sapiens skin panel (n = 4) is below the eligibility floor and motivates Chapter 5. Meeting the human floor does not establish adequate power.

3. Ten-Step Pipeline Summary

  1. Quality control applied per-tissue (mitochondrial fraction, min/max genes, Scrublet doublet removal).
  2. 163 tissue by cell-type units evaluated; 17 met the mouse eligibility floor (at least 3 mice and at least 50 cells in 3m and 18m, all in Lung or Kidney; 146 ineligible across the other 14 tissues); 14 of the 17 show aging. The floor permits analysis but does not establish adequate power.
  3. Pseudobulk differential expression per tissue by cell-type unit (PyDESeq2, 3m vs 18m, design ~ sex + age_group).
  4. Five label permutations on the top unit (Lung adventitial cell) yield a mean of 4.6 genes vs 55 observed. The within-3-month sex comparison (5 male vs 2 female mice) finds 0 significant genes but cannot rule out sex effects.
  5. Guard check on Marrow: diffusion pseudotime vs chronological age shows Spearman rho 0.016. This check finds no association in that analysis; no trajectory-based aging claim is made.
  6. Ensembl 1:1 reciprocal orthologs mapped for mouse-human and mouse-NMR.
  7. Housekeeping inflation guard monitors ribosomal and mitochondrial fraction against 0.017 background; these genes are not filtered out of the reported programs.
  8. Life-stage normalized regression and exploratory Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) evaluate NMR equivalence (not established, p = 0.329). The margin was derived from the observed mouse slope, not pre-registered.
  9. M9 human cohort arm: 261 direction-specific entries across 257 unique genes scored in 621 control donors across 11 public CELLxGENE cohorts (brain, kidney, lung). No whole-cohort rise is supported; MSSM has nominal p = 0.028 and BH q = 0.311 across the 11 cohorts. Cell-type estimates vary in direction.
  10. M10 lifespan-atlas arm (G16): the same ortholog program scored in the DLPFC Lifespan Atlas (284 control donors aged 0.2 to 97, SD 24, under AD Knowledge Portal data use agreement) using the atlas authors' per-donor, per-cell-type pseudobulk summaries. M9 averages per-cell scores before donor summaries, whereas M10 scores pseudobulk expression, so their coefficients are not identical or directly comparable. The whole-cortex available-cell-type average has slope +0.00015 per year and p = 0.362; absent cell types are excluded rather than set to zero, and varying composition makes the average descriptive. Seven cell types have same-direction nominal p < 0.05 in raw and CPM schemes, with FDR and adult-only caveats; other rows are drawn hollow and no direction is claimed. Regression aggregates only; per-donor values are controlled data.

4. Three Load-Bearing Limitations

  • Partial sex by age confounding: In the TMS droplet arm, 1-month, 24-month, and 30-month mice are male-only and 21-month mice are female-only; only the 3-month and 18-month groups include both sexes. The primary 3m vs 18m contrast therefore has both-sex replication and adjusts for sex, but sex and age cannot be separated across the full age range, and the within-young null does not rule out sex effects.
  • Outlier Scope: Naked mole-rat data are skin only, with 31% program coverage, five animals, and two group ages (Savina et al. 2022, Aging). This analysis does not include a multi-tissue in vivo single-cell atlas across the NMR lifespan.
  • Cross-Sectional Sampling: All datasets reflect cross-sectional donor cohorts, not longitudinal tracking of living individuals.
